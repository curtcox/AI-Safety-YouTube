# Ari Juels - Security Tools from Crypto for AI

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oPga-yk_UGw) · FAR․AI · 2026-06-21 · 18:59

## Description

```text
Ari Juels (Cornell University) opens with the KelpDAO breach, a Lazarus Group attack that drained over a quarter of a billion dollars after a single piece of poisoned data reached a smart contract, to argue that the crypto industry has been operating in cybersecurity's "tornado alley": autonomous code, easily monetizable exploits, and nation-state adversaries. AI systems share enough of those features that the defenses crypto has built may apply directly. He walks through two transfers. The first is Oracles, infrastructure that relays untampered data from trusted sources to programs, and whose first production deployment of confidential hardware (Town Crier on Intel SGX) actually predates most TEE work in AI. Coupled with models running in TEEs, Oracles unlock pipelines like a loan decision where a bank's approved model judges a private applicant's financials without the loan officer ever touching the data, or "πCreds," private inference credentials proving that a model has reviewed someone's documentation and judged them qualified, extensible from human credentials to attestations that a proprietary AI agent's code has been reviewed and found to lack a backdoor. The second is more speculative: a revival of N-version programming, where multiple diversified implementations of a program run in parallel and any disagreement on an input signals a bug without being weaponizable. The 2018 "Hydra" version of this idea stalled on the cost of paying multiple human developers, but frontier models can now produce the diverse implementations and specifications themselves, making it newly practical as an early-warning system for the widening gap between code written under older models and the offensive capabilities of newer ones.
```
