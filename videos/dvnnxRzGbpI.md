# Andrew Ilyas - How worried should we be about data poisoning?

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dvnnxRzGbpI) · FAR․AI · 2026-06-08 · 25:21

## Description

```text
Andrew Ilyas (CMU) takes stock of where data poisoning sits as a threat. 
Right now, defenders are winning, but his contention is that this is largely because the attacks we have just aren't very good. He lays out a taxonomy organized around who the Red Team is, what they're trying to do, and what each side can see, then walks through scenarios that scale in realism: a user fine-tuning through an API to elicit bad outputs, a malicious synthetic data generator inserted into a pre-training pipeline, an internally misaligned model propagating its misalignment into successors during automated R&D, and a misaligned model gaming the synthesis criteria fed to an otherwise controlled data pipeline. 
He then surveys two attack families. Trigger-based attacks, where co-locating a trigger with a target behavior teaches a correlation, follow a trajectory that already played out in computer vision: defenses based on data sanitization work until attackers get more clever, and Ilyas suspects this line eventually collapses into jailbreaking, since natural text already contains plenty of usable triggers. Subliminal learning attacks, where a teacher model transmits hidden properties through seemingly unrelated outputs, look structurally similar to the non-robust features work that came out of adversarial examples, and recent results suggest the early constraints (same architecture, same initialization) are loosening. Both, in his read, succeed somewhat by accident, because the underlying problem is bi-level optimization, controlling the output of someone else's optimization problem. His group's contribution is a primitive called the metagradient that lets you differentiate an attacker's objective with respect to training data and run gradient descent on the poison directly. In a vision case study, this drops a model's accuracy by 14 percentage points using 1% of the training data, against a prior best of 0.8.

Note: The opinions shared in this event are those of the speaker(s) and may not represent the views of FAR.AI or their affiliated organizations.
```
