## Description

```text
Gillian Hadfield critiques current models of AI alignment, suggesting that instead of extracting and embedding human preferences or values directly into AI systems, we should focus on aligning AI with the dynamic normative systems of human societies. Hadfield argues that human societies are organized around shared judgments and cooperative norms, which shape individual behavior, and that static documents such as constitutions are insufficient. To integrate AI into human groups safely and robustly, AI systems should be designed to understand and align with these shared normative infrastructures. The talk emphasizes that AI should be able to adapt to changes in human normative systems. Hadfield also addresses the potential need for digital institutions to aid AI in interpreting human classification systems and touches on the broader implications of AI agents potentially participating in human societal structures.

“Alignment is Social: Lessons from Human Alignment for AI“ was presented on August 6, 2025 at FAR.AI Labs Seminar.
```
