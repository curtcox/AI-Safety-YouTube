## Description

```text
Adam Gleave (FAR.AI) opens the London Alignment Workshop by arguing that the central obstacle to AI safety coordination is not the absence of solutions but the absence of a standard: without a shared, legible definition of what makes an AI system safe, companies and governments have no basis for holding each other accountable. Drawing on analogies from civil and nuclear engineering, Gleave proposes that the field adopt an engineering approach to safety, and argues that any workable standard must meet three criteria: assurance (the system is genuinely likely to be safe if the standard is followed), auditability (third parties can verify compliance without relying on trust), and efficiency (requirements are no more onerous than necessary). He outlines how this can be achieved through modularity, empirically validated rules of thumb, and rigorous end-to-end testing, illustrating each through concrete examples including layered defense pipelines, adversarial robustness scaling laws, and probe-based deception detection, and argues that the field already has tractable tools it is largely failing to use.
```
