## Description

```text
Gillian Hadfield (Johns Hopkins) makes the case that the central challenge in AI governance is not technical but structural: regulatory capacity is not keeping pace with AI capabilities, and the solution is to build a market-based ecosystem of Independent Verification Organizations (IVOs) — licensed, competing private-sector entities authorized by government to define and verify specific AI safety claims on behalf of developers and the public. Drawing on decades of regulatory theory, Hadfield lays out the legal and economic architecture that would make this work, including how existing tort liability, insurance markets, and procurement rules could generate the demand needed to fund serious safety research outside the labs, without waiting for a formal regulatory mandate.
```
