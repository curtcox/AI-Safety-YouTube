## Description

```text
Koen van der Veen (OpenMined) makes the case that AI auditing has gone from a hard sell to something organizations now request, and presents PySyft, a data governance layer that sits on top of trusted execution environments so researchers can compute on data they cannot see. The recurring problem is trust: benchmark leakage means we cannot be sure models are as good as they claim, and a prediction may not come from the model it claims to. Van der Veen walks through two patterns OpenMined has run in practice: remote data science, where a data scientist's code runs on private user logs without ever exposing them; and private-private benchmarks, where a private model and a private benchmark meet inside an enclave that executes only with buy-in from every party. He frames the destination as three pillars of oversight: confidential evaluation that turns leaderboards into closed-book exams, a registry of what each model scored, and verified inference tying a prediction back to a model with a known score.

Note: The opinions shared in this event are those of the speaker(s) and may not represent the views of FAR.AI or their affiliated organizations.
```
