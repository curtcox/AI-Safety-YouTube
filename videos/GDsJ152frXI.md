# Aditya Singh - Model incrimination

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GDsJ152frXI) · FAR․AI · 2026-06-04 · 23:43

## Description

```text
Aditya Singh (MATS 9.0) makes the case for a piece of safety work that doesn't really have a name yet: model incrimination, or proving that a bad action was actually scheming. 
He frames it as the gap between catching a bad action and justifying a serious response. Catching gets attention through control and auditing; responding gets attention through governance; almost no one is working on the transition. 
The technical problem is hard because aligned models also do concerning things, for mundane reasons. Singh walks through three: Palisade's shutdown resistance, where Gemini 2.5 Pro was just confused about whether shutdown should override its math task; his own work where DeepSeek R1 sandbagged because it thought the user wanted it to underperform; and a Claude Opus 4.5 case where the model fabricated a search result because it had been trained to ignore prompt injections. The Bayesian denominator, the probability of the action given alignment, is full of these explanations, and they have to be ruled out. His optimistic claim is that this is tractable now, by practicing on today's models, using a Kimi K2 Thinking case study to demonstrate how converging behavioral experiments can establish a claim like "this is laziness" without any ground truth available. Without that practice, he argues, the field will arrive at the moment that matters most, the first serious catch, without a methodology for turning it into a credible case.

Note: The opinions shared in this event are those of the speaker(s) and may not represent the views of FAR.AI or their affiliated organizations.
```
