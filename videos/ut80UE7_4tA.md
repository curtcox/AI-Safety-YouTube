## Description

```text
Robert Trager (Oxford Martin AI Governance Initiative) challenges the assumption that improved alignment automatically leads to safer outcomes. He argues that in a world where better control methods simply shift the equilibrium toward higher-capability, higher-risk systems, alignment and control work provides no guarantee of safety improvement — and may provide none at all. Trager outlines a four-layer governance stack: scientific identification of actionable red lines, civilian governance through international institutions (modeled on FATF and ICAO), middle-power security monitoring (IAEA-style), and unprecedented major-power verification agreements. He highlights that leading companies continue pursuing recursive improvement despite known risks, demonstrating that warnings alone don't change behavior. The framework emphasizes compute governance as a leverage point, with cloud providers serving as monitoring intermediaries similar to financial fraud detection systems. Trager's analysis reveals that each governance layer requires increasingly sophisticated verification mechanisms, with the major-power layer demanding verification capabilities beyond any historical precedent.
```
