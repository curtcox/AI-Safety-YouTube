## Description

```text
Jimmy Farrell (Pour Demain) argues that technical AI safety research has reached a point of policy readiness, and that the window for researchers to shape frameworks like the EU Code of Practice is open now in a way it wasn't a year or two ago. Civil society organizations play a critical bridging role in this process — producing policy-facing research on issues like serious incident reporting, conducting technical evaluations, and making high-level political interventions when necessary — translating field expertise into regulatory language. On the Code of Practice specifically, public transparency provisions nearly didn't make it in, and the language that did is heavily caveated, creating significant ongoing work for the safety community to demonstrate why transparency is necessary and doesn't undermine the framework's effectiveness. A broader convergence in frontier AI governance across jurisdictions makes the moment more urgent, and formal participation mechanisms — including the EU Scientific Panel and Advisory Forum — offer structured pathways for technical input into policy.
```
