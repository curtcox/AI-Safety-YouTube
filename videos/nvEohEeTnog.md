## Description

```text
Edward Hughes (Google Deepmind) argues that the standard approach to AI evaluation—which assumes we know what we're looking for—breaks down for AI systems capable of doing science autonomously. Scientific creativity operates in the space of unknown unknowns: there was no predefined rubric for Fleming's discovery of penicillin, just as there wasn't for Bach's music or Marie Curie's discovery of radium. This means evaluation frameworks are fundamentally ill-suited for assessing AI systems that do science. Instead, Hughes proposes shifting from evaluation to recognition: building systems that can identify beneficial or harmful discoveries after the fact, much like a PhD viva assesses work that couldn't have been specified in advance. He sketches a blueprint for scalable recognition systems and closes with an open question about how to balance computational resources between discovery and recognition.
```
